\subsection{The escalation target $\Delta$}
For a cheap model $s$ and an expensive model $e$ we define the marginal value of
escalation on the calibrated gold-label probability:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{s\rightarrow e}(x) \;=\; p_e(y^\star \mid x) \;-\; p_s(y^\star \mid x),
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
where $y^\star$ is the gold label. \todo{Explain why a continuous target is used
rather than a correctness flip: a $\{-1,0,+1\}$ target is dominated by
coin-flips near the decision boundary and yields a fantasy oracle. Then state
the limitation forward-reference to \S\ref{sec:limitations}: the intended
seed-averaged target could not be constructed under frozen checkpoints.}

\subsection{Representation statistics}
For each example, hidden states at layers $\{4,8,12\}$ are collected over the
non-padding token dimension, giving $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$. Let
$p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \sum_j \sigma_j^2$ be the normalised squared singular values
of the column-centred $X$, and $U$ the row-normalised $X$. We compute
\begin{align}
\text{effective rank} &= \exp\!\Big(-\textstyle\sum_i p_i \log p_i\Big),\\
\text{spectral concentration} &= p_1,\\
\text{angular dispersion} &= 1 - \big\lVert \tfrac{1}{T}\textstyle\sum_t U_t \big\rVert.
\end{align}
Three sources $\times$ three layers $\times$ three statistics gives 27 features.

\section{Finding I: Benchmark Contamination}
\label{sec:f1}

\claim{AfriXNLI is a translation of XNLI, and XNLI-trained checkpoints therefore
cannot be evaluated cleanly on its English, French, or Swahili configurations.}

Of the 1{,}050 unique English premise--hypothesis pairs in AfriXNLI,
\textbf{1{,}047 (99.7\%)} appear verbatim in the XNLI evaluation splits, and the
450-example AfriXNLI English development split is \emph{exactly} XNLI
validation.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
& \multicolumn{3}{c}{Contaminated configs (test)} & Clean \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-4}
Model & \texttt{eng} & \texttt{fra} & \texttt{swa} & 15 langs \\
\midrule
MiniLM-L6      & 0.760 & 0.720 & 0.622 & 0.410 \\
mDeBERTa-base  & 0.890 & 0.867 & 0.742 & 0.545 \\
XLM-R-large    & \textbf{1.000} & \textbf{0.995} & \textbf{0.978} & 0.523 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Accuracy on the test split. $n=600$ per contaminated configuration,
$n=9{,}000$ for the clean set. XLM-R-large is perfect on English test.}
\label{tab:contamination}
\end{table}

\claim{The contamination is split-specific.} MiniLM and mDeBERTa state training
on the XNLI \emph{development} set and memorise it accordingly---mDeBERTa scores
1.000 on AfriXNLI English \emph{dev} but only 0.890 on English \emph{test}.
XLM-R-large's 1.000 on English test and 0.978 on Swahili test is
\claim{consistent with training on the XNLI test split}, though its model card
does not specify which split was used.

\todo{Table~\ref{tab:cards}: which model saw which split, card quotation, and
observed accuracy. Make the alignment visually obvious --- this is the figure a
reader will remember.}

\paragraph{Wording constraint.} For the 15 African configurations this is
\emph{benchmark-lineage} contamination: the surface strings are translations,
but the underlying examples were seen in other languages. It is not verbatim
leakage, and must not be described as such.

26.0\%, flat 22.7\%), and only in the 11/15 languages where the cheap rung
clears chance.

\section{Finding IV: A Real Signal That Does Not Yield a Useful Router}
\label{sec:f4}

\claim{One geometry statistic has a highly stable association with $\Delta$ that
confers no practical routing advantage over a confidence threshold.}

\subsection{The association is robust}
For \texttt{mDeBERTa L12 effective rank} on the MiniLM$\rightarrow$mDeBERTa pair
($n=9{,}000$), the within-language partial Spearman is $\rho = -0.127$, 95\% CI
$[-0.147, -0.106]$ (Fisher-$z$), Holm-adjusted $p = 3.88\times 10^{-32}$,
sign-consistent in 12/15 languages.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Stability test & Result \\
\midrule
Bootstrap, 300$\times$ within-language resample & $-0.127$, 95\% $[-0.142,-0.111]$ \\
Subsample 25\%, 40 seeds & $-0.122 \pm 0.019$ \\
Subsample 50\%, 40 seeds & $-0.129 \pm 0.011$ \\
Leave-one-language-out & $[-0.138, -0.117]$ \\
Label permutation, 20$\times$ & $-0.001$, $\max|\rho| = 0.026$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{All resampling variants remain negative; the permutation null is
centred at zero with a maximum magnitude five times smaller than the effect.
Variance explained is nonetheless only $\approx 1.6\%$.}
\label{tab:stability}
\end{table}

\subsection{It loses to confidence under matched compute}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrr}
\toprule
Budget & Cheap & Random & Conf. & Geom. & G+C & Oracle & All-exp. \\
\midrule
20\% & 0.426 & 0.461 & \textbf{0.466} & 0.460 & 0.463 & 0.555 & 0.577 \\
40\% & 0.426 & 0.493 & \textbf{0.506} & 0.498 & 0.498 & 0.632 & 0.577 \\
60\% & 0.426 & 0.518 & \textbf{0.543} & 0.524 & 0.526 & 0.678 & 0.577 \\
80\% & 0.426 & 0.546 & \textbf{0.564} & 0.556 & 0.554 & 0.661 & 0.577 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Leave-one-language-out routing accuracy at matched compute budget,
11 viable ladder languages. ``Conf.'' escalates the least-confident examples;
``Geom.'' is a ridge regression on all 18 geometry features.}
\label{tab:router}
\end{table}

The confidence$-$geometry gap is positive at every budget and every ridge
penalty $\lambda \in \{1, 10, 100, 1000\}$, ranging from $+0.006$ to $+0.019$,
with confidence winning in 7--10 of 11 held-out languages.

\paragraph{Wording constraint.} At the 20\% and 40\% budgets the per-language
standard deviation ($\approx 0.015$) exceeds the mean gap ($\approx 0.007$).
\claim{The correct statement is that geometry never beats confidence at any
budget or penalty, and is clearly worse at moderate budgets.} It must not be
stated as confidence substantially outperforming geometry everywhere.

\paragraph{Unexploited headroom.} The oracle reaches $0.678$ at the 60\% budget
against $0.577$ for always-expensive: the routing problem is genuinely
learnable, and neither signal captures it.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\todo{Do not bury these. Reviewers will find them.}

\paragraph{$\Delta$ is not seed-averaged.} \claim{The intended target was
$\Delta$ averaged over multiple fine-tuning seeds; frozen off-the-shelf
checkpoints made this impossible, so part of the variance in
Equation~\ref{eq:delta} is checkpoint idiosyncrasy rather than example
difficulty.} This cuts specifically against Finding~IV: a cleaner target might
prove more predictable. \todo{This is the single most significant methodological
gap. State it in these terms.}

\paragraph{Benchmark lineage affects the clean languages too.} They are
translations of XNLI examples the checkpoints saw in other languages.

\paragraph{Calibration touched the same lineage.} Temperature was fitted on
African development splits which share XNLI ancestry. All reported results are
on test and temperature is a single scalar per model, but the pipeline is not
contamination-free.

\paragraph{Encoder-only.} No decoder or instruction-tuned rung was evaluated;
conclusions may not transfer to LLM test-time-compute settings.

\paragraph{Router coverage.} Restricted to the 11/15 languages where the cheap
rung clears chance.

\paragraph{Geometry definition.} Per-example statistics over a single sequence's
tokens are partly length-determined by construction; length is controlled
statistically rather than by design.

\paragraph{Single $\Delta$ pair.} Findings III and IV both rest on
MiniLM$\rightarrow$mDeBERTa.
